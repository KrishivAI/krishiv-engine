The final phase of a large hash aggregate allocates each partition's rows and aggregate states once, sized from its group estimate, instead of growing them by doubling, and sizes its key index to that estimate instead of the next power of two above it. TPC-H q18 at SF100 runs 16% faster warm with 19% less peak memory, and ClickBench q33 runs 15% faster with 5% less (server, ABBA x3; q01 and q13 unchanged). A task the memory pool cannot grant that up front merges as before. `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` reports `row_final_presized`.
A thread-local aggregate table whose keys are nearly all new now switches to append-only after 2^17 input rows instead of 2^20: ClickBench q14 uses 12% fewer CPU cycles (17% faster warm) and TPC-H q13 at SF100 5% fewer.
