The `krishiv-session` library gains service principals with trusted impersonation: a principal table (`krishiv.auth.principals.<name>.privileges|impersonates|expires`, names may contain dots), signed act-as assertions, and `ServerAuth::impersonate` / `impersonate_signed`, by which a service such as a portal acts for an end user. The end user's role is capped at the service's, `expires` ends a principal's rights for every login method (a static service token needs one), and act-as never crosses tenants. Every refusal is audited and counted; `system.sessions` and `system.queries` show who acted for whom (`delegated_by`). `krishiv server` also hardens JWT logins (a lifetime cap, a required `exp`, the token id in the audit record, tokens carrying `act` refused as logins). The Connect transport that reads the `x-krishiv-act-as-*` headers arrives in a later PR; until then nothing on the wire calls the new APIs.
